Racetrack

This app collects no data about you. Your position, your flown holds and your settings stay on your device, and nothing is sent to us or to anyone else.

What this app stores

The app keeps a few small values on your device only. One is your best streak in the Entry Trainer: the longest run of entries you have answered correctly in a row, kept so the trainer can show it again the next time you open the app. The other two are the app version and the date of the last time the app asked whether you would like to rate it, kept so that it asks at most once per version and never twice within 120 days. Starting with version 2.2, the Live tab also keeps four settings: whether the spoken callouts are on, whether the chart is shown, how bright it is, and whether the map turns track up.

Holds you fly on the Live tab. When you end a hold, the app saves it on your device so you can look at it again under Past holds: the fix and the hold you set up, the entry called and the entry flown, the lap times, and your track as a list of positions, times, altitudes and speeds, about one per second. These files never leave the app. Like any app's data, they are included in your own iCloud or computer backup of the device if you make one. You can delete any saved hold with a swipe under Past holds, and deleting the app removes them all.

Nothing else about your progress is saved. Lesson position, quiz scores, the holds you have worked in the trainers and which entries you got right live in memory while the app is open, and start fresh the next time you launch it. The app has no account and no identifier.

Earlier versions kept two different values for the rating request: a count of correct entries, and a note that the request had been made. Version 2.1 deletes both the first time it opens.

There is no control inside the app for clearing the streak, the rating values or the Live settings. Deleting the app removes them.

Location

The app asks for your location the first time you open the Live tab, and uses it only while that tab is in use. It shows your airplane on the map and works out your course to a fix you pick, so a hold can be built around the entry you want. Fly it live follows your airplane around the hold you set up: it calls the entry, times the legs and draws your track. It keeps following while the screen is locked, so the callouts keep coming, and it stops when you tap End hold. Your position is never sent anywhere. The Demo on the Live tab uses a simulated airplane and needs no location at all. You can turn location off for Racetrack at any time in the iPhone's Settings.

Racetrack Pro

Flying a live hold on your own GPS is part of Racetrack Pro, a monthly subscription with a 3-day free trial. Everything that was free before version 2.2 stays free. Apple handles the subscription and the payment; the app only asks the App Store whether this Apple Account has an active Pro subscription, and never sees your name, email or payment details. The subscription renews every month until you cancel it in Settings > your name > Subscriptions. The Terms of Use are Apple's standard license agreement.

Training aid, not for navigation

Racetrack is for practicing holds. It is not certified for navigation and must not be used as your primary means of navigation. Fly by your certified instruments, your clearance and ATC. The app's GPS position, leg timing and callouts can be wrong or late.

What this app does not do

  • No account, sign-in, or email address
  • No analytics, tracking, or advertising
  • No third-party SDKs of any kind
  • No network requests of its own. Every lesson, diagram, hold, quiz question, the IFR chart and the list of fixes are inside the app. The street map under the chart on the Live tab is Apple Maps, which iOS loads from Apple; with no connection the chart and your track still draw. The App Store pages on the More screen and the subscription are Apple's too.
  • No camera, microphone, contacts, photos or health access
  • No subscriptions

Links out of the app

The Lessons menu has four items. Quiz opens the quiz inside the app. Support and Privacy Policy open this page in your browser. More from AutoPilot opens a screen inside the app that lists the other AutoPilot apps, repeats the Privacy Policy and Support links, and links to flywithautopilot.com in your browser.

Tapping one of the other apps opens its App Store page inside Racetrack, or in the App Store app if the page cannot load there. That page is Apple's: the App Store loads it, and Racetrack is not told what you do there. At the end of a quiz, Share Racetrack opens the iOS share sheet with a link to this app's App Store listing, and you choose where it goes, if anywhere.

These App Store links can carry a campaign label, more-racetrack or share-racetrack, which lets App Store Connect count downloads by the kind of link they came from. The label names the link, never you, and nothing about you travels with it.

The rating request is handled by iOS itself, in Apple's own dialog: the app asks the system to show it and receives no answer back, so it never learns whether you rated it or what you said. It asks only after a success (five correct entries in a row, or a quiz score of 80 percent or better), and iOS decides whether to show the dialog at all.

Support

Questions, bugs, or an entry you think the app has wrong: jpostflight@gmail.com. Say which screen it was and which hold: the fix, the inbound course, the turn direction and your heading. Include your device and iOS version too.

Racetrack teaches holding pattern entries: reading a hold out of a clearance or off a chart, picking direct, parallel or teardrop, and flying the wind correction. Use it to learn the method, not as the authority. The entry procedures it teaches are the ones the AIM recommends rather than regulation, the sector boundaries it draws are a teaching aid rather than a tolerance, and the hold you are actually cleared into is the one ATC gave you or the one printed on the chart. Fly the clearance you have.
