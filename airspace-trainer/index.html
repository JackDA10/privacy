Airspace Trainer

This app collects no data about you and makes no network requests of its own.

What this app stores

Three things, on your device only. None of it leaves the device, and deleting the app removes all of it.

  • Which units you have attempted and passed, which is your place in the course (stored as airspacetrainer.units.v1). The Learn screen has a Reset progress control at the bottom, which appears once there is something to clear.
  • Which topics you have answered incorrectly, so the drill can bring those back to you (stored as airspacetrainer.weakness.v1). To clear it, open the Drill tab, tap Progress, and use Clear history.
  • Starting with version 1.0.1, when the app last asked you for a rating: the version that asked (stored as AutoPilot.review.lastAskedVersion) and the date it asked (stored as AutoPilot.review.lastAskedDate). The app asks only right after you pass a unit, at most once per version and no more than once in 120 days, and these two records are how it keeps to that. They hold nothing else, and only deleting the app clears them.

The course record and the drill history are kept apart deliberately, and clearing one leaves the other alone. Starting the course again and starting the drill again are different things to want.

What this app does not do

  • No account, sign-in, or email address
  • No analytics, tracking, or advertising
  • No third-party SDKs of any kind
  • No network requests of its own. Every lesson, diagram, scenario and regulation is inside the app, and it works fully in airplane mode. What can leave the device is listed under Links out of the app below, and each item there starts with something you tap or pass.
  • No location, camera, microphone, contacts, photos or health access. The app has no permission prompt at all.
  • No purchases or subscriptions

Links out of the app

  • Regulations. Every rule the app teaches carries its citation, and tapping one opens that section of the eCFR, the US government's electronic Code of Federal Regulations, in your browser.
  • Privacy Policy and Support. On the More screen (the Drill tab, then Progress, then More from AutoPilot), these open this page in your browser.
  • flywithautopilot.com. The last row of the More screen opens it in your browser.
  • Other AutoPilot apps. Starting with version 1.0.1, tapping one of them on the More screen opens Apple's App Store page for that app inside Airspace Trainer, rather than in a browser. The page is Apple's, and iOS loads it. If it cannot load, the same listing opens in the App Store instead.
  • Rating the app. Starting with version 1.0.1, Apple's own rating prompt may appear shortly after you pass a unit, within the limits above. iOS draws it, and a rating you choose to give goes to Apple.
  • Sharing the app. Starting with version 1.0.1, a Share Airspace Trainer button appears when you pass a unit. It opens the iOS share sheet with a link to the app's App Store listing, and nothing is sent unless you pick somewhere to send it.

The app itself sends nothing with any of these: no identifier, no progress, nothing about you. Once a page is open in your browser or in Apple's App Store view, that site's or Apple's privacy practices apply rather than this one's.

Support

Questions, bugs, or a regulation you think the app has wrong: admin@flywithautopilot.com. Say which screen and which citation, and include your device and iOS version.

Airspace Trainer teaches the US VFR airspace system from one picture. Use it to learn the structure, not as the authority. Figures shown as typical (Class B to 10,000 MSL, Class C to 4,000 AGL, Class D to 2,500 AGL) are labeled that way because a specific area's real dimensions are on the sectional chart and in FAA Order JO 7400.11. Read the chart for the airspace you are actually flying in.
