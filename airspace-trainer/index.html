Airspace Trainer

This app collects no data about you and makes no network connections of its own.

What this app stores

On your device only: which lessons you have completed and passed, and which topics you have answered incorrectly, so the drill can bring those back to you. Nothing else. It never leaves the device, the Progress tab has a control that clears it, and deleting the app removes it.

What this app does not do

  • No account, sign-in, or email address
  • No analytics, tracking, or advertising
  • No third-party SDKs of any kind
  • No network requests — every lesson, diagram, scenario and regulation is inside the app, and it works fully in airplane mode
  • No location, camera, microphone, contacts, photos or health access — the app has no permission prompt at all
  • No purchases or subscriptions

Links to regulations

Every rule the app teaches carries its citation, and tapping one hands that link to your browser to open the relevant page of the eCFR — the US government's electronic Code of Federal Regulations. The app itself sends nothing: no identifier, no progress, nothing about you travels with that link. Once the page is open in your browser, that site's privacy practices apply rather than this one's.

Support

Questions, bugs, or a regulation you think the app has wrong: jackdeeganpost@gmail.com. Say which screen and which citation, and include your device and iOS version.

Airspace Trainer teaches the US VFR airspace system from one picture. Use it to learn the structure, not as the authority: figures shown as typical — Class B to 10,000 MSL, Class C to 4,000 AGL, Class D to 2,500 AGL — are labelled that way because a specific area's real dimensions are on the sectional chart and in FAA Order JO 7400.11. Read the chart for the airspace you are actually flying in.
