Airspace Trainer

This app collects no data about you and makes no network connections of its own.

What this app stores

Two things, on your device only, and each has its own control that clears it:

  • Which units you have attempted and passed — your place in the course. The Learn screen has a Reset progress control at the bottom, which appears once there is something to clear.
  • Which topics you have answered incorrectly, so the drill can bring those back to you. The Progress tab has a Clear history control.

They are kept apart deliberately, and clearing one leaves the other alone — starting the course again and starting the drill again are different things to want. Nothing else is stored, none of it leaves the device, and deleting the app removes both.

What this app does not do

  • No account, sign-in, or email address
  • No analytics, tracking, or advertising
  • No third-party SDKs of any kind
  • No network requests — every lesson, diagram, scenario and regulation is inside the app, and it works fully in airplane mode
  • No location, camera, microphone, contacts, photos or health access — the app has no permission prompt at all
  • No purchases or subscriptions

Links out of the app

Every rule the app teaches carries its citation, and tapping one hands that link to your browser to open the relevant page of the eCFR — the US government's electronic Code of Federal Regulations. The More from AutoPilot screen works the same way, linking to the App Store and to flywithautopilot.com. The app itself sends nothing: no identifier, no progress, nothing about you travels with that link. Once the page is open in your browser, that site's privacy practices apply rather than this one's.

Support

Questions, bugs, or a regulation you think the app has wrong: admin@flywithautopilot.com. Say which screen and which citation, and include your device and iOS version.

Airspace Trainer teaches the US VFR airspace system from one picture. Use it to learn the structure, not as the authority: figures shown as typical — Class B to 10,000 MSL, Class C to 4,000 AGL, Class D to 2,500 AGL — are labelled that way because a specific area's real dimensions are on the sectional chart and in FAA Order JO 7400.11. Read the chart for the airspace you are actually flying in.
